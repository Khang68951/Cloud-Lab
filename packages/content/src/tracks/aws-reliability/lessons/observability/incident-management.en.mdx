Incidents will happen. Great teams differ in that they **detect fast, mitigate fast, and learn every time**.

## The incident lifecycle

```
Detect ─► Triage ─► Mitigate ─► Recover ─► Postmortem ─► Improve
(alarm)  (severity) (rollback,   (normal    (blameless)   (owned
                     shift traffic) service)               action items)
```

## Golden rules

- **Mitigate first, find the cause later**: roll back, flip a feature flag, shift traffic. Debugging comes after.
- **Clear severity levels**: SEV1 = broad customer impact, SEV3 = a minor feature broken. Severity decides who is paged and how fast.
- **Clear roles**: the *incident commander* coordinates, the *communications lead* updates stakeholders and customers, engineers focus on the fix.
- **Runbooks**: pre-written steps for known incidents — ideally automated (SSM Automation).
- **One shared channel** and a **timeline**: record every decision with its time.

## Blameless postmortems

A good postmortem answers:
1. What happened (timeline), and what was the impact (how many users, how long)?
2. Why did the system **allow** it, and why was it not detected sooner? Use the "5 whys".
3. Concrete action items, **with owners and due dates**.

The right question is not "who caused it?" but "why could someone following the process still cause this?".

## AWS tools

**AWS Health Dashboard** (AWS-side events), **Systems Manager Incident Manager** (on-call, escalation, runbooks), **CloudWatch** (alarms and dashboards), **Fault Injection Service** (rehearsals).
