These three challenges are **real** problems that large products had to solve, described publicly in the [System Design Primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer) and the AWS Solutions Library.

| Challenge | Source | The hardest part |
|---|---|---|
| **Twitter home timeline** | System Design Primer | 20× more reads than writes → precompute timelines (fan-out on write) |
| **Video on demand** | AWS Solution "Video on Demand on AWS" | Transcode once, deliver millions of times → the CDN decides the bill |
| **Virtual waiting room** | AWS Solution "Virtual Waiting Room on AWS" | Traffic jumps 30× in seconds → admit at a steady pace through a queue |

## The first question is always: read/write ratio and traffic shape

- **Reads ≫ writes** (timelines, video): do the heavy work **at write time** so reads are cheap — caches, CDNs, precomputed data.
- **Sharp, short peaks** (ticket drops): do not scale the backend for the peak — **flatten** it with a queue.
- **Slow work** (transcoding): take it off the request path with events (dashed edges).
