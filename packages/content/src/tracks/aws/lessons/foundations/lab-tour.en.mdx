In CloudLab you learn by **designing systems and seeing what they cost, how fast they are and how they break**. Your main tool is the **Architecture Lab**: a draw.io-style canvas with an AWS simulator behind it.

## The Lab has 3 areas

| Area | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Left — service palette** | Drag an AWS service (EC2, S3, RDS…) onto the canvas, or click to add it |
| **Centre — canvas** | Place and **connect** services: hover a node and drag from a dot to another node |
| **Right — pricing & review** | Monthly cost, latency, availability, bottlenecks and AWS Well-Architected warnings |

Every time you add, connect or reconfigure something, all the numbers on the right **recalculate instantly**.

## Reading a node

- **Bottom left**: that service's monthly cost.
- **Bottom right**: load at peak. <span className="text-success">Green</span> is fine, <span className="text-warning">amber</span> is running hot (≥ 80%), <span className="text-danger">red</span> is overloaded and failing requests. `∞` means the service scales automatically.
- **Arrows** show requests/second. **Dashed** arrows are asynchronous (through a queue), off the user's request path.

<Callout type="tip">
With nothing selected, the **Scenario** tab lets you change traffic (requests/second), the peak multiplier, the read/write ratio and the region. Try doubling the traffic and see what breaks first.
</Callout>

## Chaos — break it on purpose

The **Chaos** menu takes a whole Availability Zone offline, spikes traffic 10×, or kills individual nodes. A good design **survives** these.

<Callout type="warning">
Prices in the Lab are **reference prices** (On-Demand, no tax or free tier). They are accurate enough to compare options; use the AWS Pricing Calculator for real decisions.
</Callout>
