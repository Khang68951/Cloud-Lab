Encryption on AWS has two sides: **at rest** and **in transit**. At the centre of both is **AWS KMS**, the key management service.

## In transit vs at rest

| | In transit | At rest |
|---|---|---|
| Protects against | Eavesdropping on the wire | Someone reading disks, snapshots, backups |
| Tools | TLS (free certificates from **ACM** for ALB/CloudFront/API Gateway) | S3, EBS, RDS, DynamoDB… encryption via KMS |
| Enforce | `aws:SecureTransport` condition — HTTPS only | On by default (S3 and DynamoDB are encrypted); check with SCPs/Config |

## KMS key types

| Type | Managed by | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **AWS owned** | AWS, invisible to you | Default for many services, free |
| **AWS managed** (`aws/s3`, `aws/rds`…) | AWS, in your account | Simple, but you cannot edit the key policy |
| **Customer managed** | You | You need control over who uses it, cross-account sharing, own rotation, separate audit. $1/month each |

## Envelope encryption

KMS does not encrypt your large files directly. Instead:

1. KMS generates a **data key**.
2. The service encrypts the data locally with it.
3. The data key is **encrypted under your KMS key** and stored next to the data.
4. To read: KMS decrypts the data key, which then decrypts the data.

<Callout type="key">
For KMS, **the key policy is the root of trust**. An IAM policy allowing `kms:Decrypt` is not enough unless the key policy allows it or delegates to the account's IAM.
</Callout>

## Cost and tips

- $0.03 per 10,000 requests. S3 with SSE-KMS calls KMS **per object** — enable **S3 Bucket Keys** to cut requests by up to ~99%.
- Turn on automatic key rotation; old data stays readable.
- To **delete data by destroying the key** (crypto-shredding), use a separate customer managed key per customer or tenant.
