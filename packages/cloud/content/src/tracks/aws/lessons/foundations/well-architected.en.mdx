The **AWS Well-Architected Framework** is the checklist AWS architects use to review any design. The Lab's **Review** tab is a small version of it.

## The 6 pillars

| Pillar | Core question | Typical practices |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational Excellence** | Can we deploy, monitor and improve safely? | IaC, small reversible changes, runbooks, post-incident learning |
| **Security** | Are data and systems protected? | Least privilege, encryption, security at every layer, traceability |
| **Reliability** | Do we recover from failure and handle load changes? | Multi-AZ, self-healing, tested backups, limited blast radius |
| **Performance Efficiency** | Do we use resources efficiently as demand changes? | Right service types, caches, CDNs, measure before optimising |
| **Cost Optimization** | Do we pay only for what we need? | Rightsizing, the right pricing model, cost visibility per team/product |
| **Sustainability** | Do we minimise resources and energy? | High utilisation, managed services, deleting unused data |

## The pillars trade off

- Multi-AZ improves **reliability** but adds **cost**.
- A cache improves **performance** but adds **operational** complexity (invalidation, stale data).
- Encrypting everything improves **security** at a little latency and KMS cost.

<Callout type="key">
No design scores perfectly on all six. Make trade-offs **on purpose**, based on business impact: a payments system favours reliability; an internal prototype favours cost.
</Callout>

## Using it in practice

- The **AWS Well-Architected Tool** (free, in the console) asks pillar-by-pillar questions and produces a list of risks to fix.
- **Lenses** extend it for specific workloads: Serverless, SaaS, Analytics…
- In the Lab, every Review finding is tagged with its pillar — use them as a checklist before you submit.
