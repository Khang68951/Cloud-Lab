Every system-design problem can follow the same process. It stops you from skipping important steps and tells you where you are in a discussion.

## 6 steps

| # | Step | You should come out with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Clarify requirements** | Core features; non-functional needs: scale, latency, availability, consistency, budget |
| 2 | **Back-of-envelope estimates** | Requests/second (average and peak), storage per year, bandwidth, read/write ratio |
| 3 | **API and data** | A few key endpoints; what the data looks like and how it is queried (access patterns) |
| 4 | **High-level architecture** | The simplest diagram that satisfies steps 1–3 |
| 5 | **Deep dives** | Bottlenecks, hot spots, failures (AZ, dependencies), cost; fix and redraw |
| 6 | **Trade-offs and evolution** | Why this design, what breaks at ×10 and ×100, what you would change |

## Estimates: numbers worth memorising

- A day ≈ **86,400 seconds** (≈ 10⁵ for quick maths). A month ≈ **2.6 million seconds**.
- 1 request/second, steadily ≈ **2.6 million requests/month**.
- Peaks are usually **2–5×** the average; special events can be ×10.
- Requests/second ≈ daily active users × requests per user ÷ 86,400.

<Callout type="key">
**Start simple, then add**: add a cache, a queue or multi-region only when a specific requirement demands it. Every component has a price — money, latency, and one more thing that can fail.
</Callout>
