Video is most of the internet's traffic. Designing a video platform really means designing **two pipelines**: getting video in (writes, asynchronous, CPU-heavy) and playing it (reads, enormous, bandwidth-heavy).

## Upload and processing pipeline

```
Creator ─► API: get presigned URL ─► multipart upload straight to S3 (raw)
                                         │ S3 event
                                         ▼
                      EventBridge / SQS ─► MediaConvert: transcode to many bitrates (HLS/DASH)
                                         ▼
                               S3 (segments + manifests) ─► DB update: "ready" ─► notify
```

- Transcoding takes minutes — it must be **asynchronous**.
- **Adaptive bitrate**: each video has 240p … 4K renditions cut into 2–6 s segments; the player switches quality with bandwidth.

## Playback pipeline

```
Viewer ─► CloudFront (segments cached at the edge, hit ratio > 95%) ─► S3 (origin, with Origin Shield)
```

- Segments are **immutable**, so they cache perfectly; the origin sees a few percent of requests.
- **Signed URLs or cookies** restrict who can watch; DRM for paid content.
- Live streams: MediaLive + MediaPackage, then CloudFront.

## Cost: egress is everything

| Component | Example: 1 M views/day × 300 MB |
|---|---|
| Data transfer | 9 PB/month — most of the bill |
| Storage | Renditions × catalogue; lifecycle old videos to cheaper tiers |
| Transcoding | Per video minute, once per video |

Cost levers: better codecs (H.265, AV1: 30–50% smaller), CloudFront volume or contract pricing, and transcoding 4K only for genuinely popular videos.

<Callout type="tip">
Lab challenge: serve 200 segments/s of 1 MB each, with no server fleet, low latency and the bill within budget. Watch how the egress line changes with and without the CDN.
</Callout>
