**FinOps** is a way of working where engineering, finance and the business share responsibility for cloud spend. Tools are part of it; **who owns the number** matters more.

## The three FinOps phases

| Phase | Goal | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Inform** | Who spends what, on what | Tags, accounts per team, dashboards, showback |
| **Optimize** | Cut waste, raise efficiency | Rightsizing, commitments, architecture |
| **Operate** | Make it a habit | Budgets, monthly reviews, KPIs |

## The foundation: cost allocation

- **Multi-account** (AWS Organizations): one account per team or environment — the cleanest allocation.
- **Cost allocation tags**: `team`, `env`, `service`, `cost-center`. **Activate** them in Billing, and enforce them with tag policies, SCPs or IaC checks.
- **Cost Categories**: group accounts and tags into business-meaningful buckets.
- **Showback** (show teams their costs), then **chargeback** (charge their budgets).

## Catch problems early

- **AWS Budgets**: thresholds per account, tag or service; alert on **forecast**, not just actuals; budget actions to block automatically.
- **Cost Anomaly Detection**: ML finds unusual spend within hours.

## Measure the right thing: unit economics

A growing bill is not necessarily bad. Track **unit cost**: $/order, $/active user, $/GB processed. If volume grew 80% and the bill only 40%, you are doing well.

<Callout type="key">
Bring cost into **engineering workflows**: estimate cost in design reviews, show cost in pull requests (e.g. Infracost), and put unit cost next to latency and error rate on dashboards.
</Callout>
