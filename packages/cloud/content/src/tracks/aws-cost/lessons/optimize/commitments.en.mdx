If you know you will use a certain amount of compute for 1–3 years, AWS discounts it in exchange for that **commitment** — sometimes by over 70%.

## Types of commitment

| Type | You commit to | Applies to | Max discount |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Compute Savings Plans** | $/hour of compute spend | EC2 (any family, any Region), Fargate, Lambda | ~66% |
| **EC2 Instance Savings Plans** | $/hour for one family in one Region | e.g. m7g in us-east-1, any size | ~72% |
| **Reserved Instances** | A specific configuration | EC2, and **RDS, ElastiCache, OpenSearch, Redshift** | ~72% |
| **SageMaker Savings Plans** | $/hour | SageMaker | ~64% |
| **Database Savings Plans** (since Dec 2025) | $/hour, 1-year term | Aurora, RDS, DynamoDB, ElastiCache, DocumentDB, Neptune… (any engine, Region) | ~35% (instances ~20%) |

Terms of 1 or 3 years; *all upfront*, *partial* or *no upfront*. The more you prepay, the deeper the discount.

## How much to commit?

```
$/hour
100 ┤        ╭──╮        ╭──╮
    │      ╭─╯  ╰─╮    ╭─╯  ╰─╮    ← variable part: on-demand / Spot
 40 ┤──────┴──────┴────┴──────┴──  ← the floor: commit a Savings Plan here
    └───────────────────────────── time
```

- Commit to the **steady floor**, not the average. The rest runs on-demand or Spot.
- **Utilisation** = how much of the commitment is used (aim ~100%). **Coverage** = how much eligible spend is discounted (often aim 70–90%).
- Commit in **small layers** (add some each quarter) rather than one huge purchase.

<Callout type="key">
The right order: **remove waste → rightsize → then commit**. Committing before optimising locks waste in for 1–3 years.
</Callout>
